# Contributing to the material

Given the size of the material, minor errors and occasional omissions may occur in the labs or other course materials.

If you come across one and decide you'd like to help your fellow students, send a [pull request](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/how-tos/create-pull-requests/creating-a-pull-request-from-a-fork) to this site's [repo](https://github.com/bmeviaumb11/skalazhato). Before submitting your changes, try them out on your own [fork](https://docs.github.com/en/pull-requests/how-tos/work-with-forks/fork-a-repo) - you'll find instructions for this in the [readme](https://github.com/bmeviaumb11/skalazhato/blob/master/README.md).

The source code of the lecture slides is currently not public. If you find an error in the lecture material, report it by [creating an issue](https://docs.github.com/en/issues/tracking-your-work-with-issues/using-issues/creating-an-issue#creating-an-issue-from-a-repository), also in this repo.

Previously published materials that have not yet been updated for the given semester (marked with *Not updated for the xxx semester!*) may naturally contain outdated parts; these do not need to be reported separately.

!!! quote "Bonus points for fixing the notes"

    Following the example of other courses, we'd also like to give bonus points for open-source contributions to the notes.
    Students currently taking the course can earn points for their contributions.

    During the semester, bonus points can be earned for fixes that meaningfully improve the quality of the lab notes on GitHub, beyond trivial 1-2 letter typo fixes.
    E.g.: fixing a significant number of typos, clarifications, adding illustrations, or even writing a whole supplementary note (naturally not with the same point value).

    Of course, we're also happy to accept single-letter typo fixes without points, which are also perfect as a warm-up.
